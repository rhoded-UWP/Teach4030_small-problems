Take the smallest step that works.

Twelve ordinary classroom moments. For each one, pick the response you would actually try first, then compare it against where that move sits on the intrusion ladder and what it teaches the student about getting your attention.

1. NonverbalSignal, look, planned ignoring, reinforce a peer
2. ProximityMove near, touch the desk, keep teaching
3. Private verbalQuiet redirect, restate the expectation to one student
4. Public directionNamed correction, stated choice, brief conference
5. RemovalLoss of access, seat change, office, call home

What do you do first?

How to use this in a role play

Partner A plays the teacher and responds in real time before opening the card's feedback. Partner B plays the student and decides in advance whether the behavior is about attention, escape, or a missing skill, without telling A. After the exchange, both look at the ladder together and ask one question: did the response cost more attention, time, or dignity than the behavior required?
